# NFL75_PROMO_CLASS_001

Promotional reads were being scored as program. That is a real difference in the false-mute total, and it leaves the false-mute rate about where it was.

The historical labels stay as published. Gold is still commercial or program. The second classification is `overlay.json`. The consumer column is a scenario: a promotional read is wanted suppression, and a mute of it is not a false mute. A mixed mention, a dropped pass, and a studio desk stay program under both columns. Category policy still keeps a show promo until someone opts to mute it. This column is that opt-in. It is not a new default.

The detector is the frozen 10-second mean at 0.163, replayed from NFL75_BOOTH_HOLD_001. Nothing was refit. Speakers were not moved. The television was not changed. The research column matches that replay before any second is reclassified.

## Verdict

**MEASURED.** The distinction belongs in the score. It does not explain the false-mute rate.

On the unprotected oracle, moving the clean promotional reads out of program takes October from 70 episodes to 65, and from 32.3 to 30.6 per program hour. September goes from 37 to 36, and from 22.4 to 22.0. Hidden program falls from 618 seconds to 480 on October, and from 473 to 425 on September. The caps are still under 1 episode an hour, under 10 seconds, and under 5 hidden seconds an hour. The worst remaining cuts are 25 seconds and 31 seconds.

Commercial coverage stays 98.6% and 98.5%. No break is missed. A late return is untouched: September's causal worst stays 83.6 seconds at 7020–7104 under both columns.

## What the 137 and 107 seconds are

October's 137.2 seconds are five promotional reads, and the consumer column moves all five: Crucial Catch (33s), the State Farm halftime bumper (6.2s), Amex (54s), Reacher (41s), and the Matthew Lillard bumper (3s). The class clock is 141.2 seconds. Four of those seconds are the short gaps the scorer had already counted as heard inside the reads. Every covered second of the class was muted.

September's published 107.2 seconds are not all the same kind of audio. The consumer column moves 47.8: Chili's (34s) and the Allstate presenting sting (13.8s). The other 59.4 seconds stay false mutes, because the same span contains programming:

| Span | Seconds | What is in the span |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| 1902–1917 | 14s | A Prime promo, then Brian Gutekunst is named as GM |
| 2922–2938 | 16s | A Sony line, then the play call |
| 7470–7473 | 3s | "That's Cartman." No sponsor is named |
| 7489–7515 | 26.4s | A tour announcement, then the national anthem |

There is no word clock to split the Sony line from the play, or the promo from the GM. Leaving the whole span as a false mute is the conservative reading. Treating all 107.2 seconds as wanted suppression, which is the earlier bucket, would take September from 37 episodes to 33 and from 22.4 to 20.4 per hour. The worst cut would still be 31 seconds.

The Thursday Nightcap open at 9803–9822 says "presented by Allstate" and then talks about the Buccaneers. It stays program. It was not part of the 137 seconds.

Categories were assigned from the first 20 seconds of each episode. Crucial Catch, Amex, Reacher, and Chili's continue past that window, and those tails move with the episode because that is how the 137 and 107 were counted. A later listen can split a tail without editing gold.
