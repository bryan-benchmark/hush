# Boundary landmark fingerprints

The two-second spectrum at these boundaries already matched ordinary football. This pass is the landmark match that result left open. The matcher is audfprint at the published settings, and the accept count stays 200. A window is enrolled only after its own game has been scored. RedZone is not enrolled. The count was not lowered. The live mute was not changed.

Three windows, each two seconds:

| Window | Where it sits |
| --- | --- |
| Before the start | Ends at the labeled cut. This is the outro. |
| After the start | Begins at the labeled cut. This is the first sound of the break. |
| Before the end | Ends at the labeled return. This is the last sound of the break. |

A copy of one Sept 9 opening matches itself at 299 of 299 landmarks, so a real repeat of these two seconds can clear 200. None did.

Every cell at count 200 is zero, on the breaks and on the game samples. The outro, which is the sound just before the cut, never scores above 12. On Oct 11 afternoon a game sample scores 21 against earlier outros, higher than every outro on that tape.

The first two seconds of the break are the least weak window. Sept 13 reaches 36, Oct 8 reaches 33, and Oct 11 night reaches 73. Those are not copies. The same window's game samples reach 16 on Oct 11 afternoon, above that tape's best break. The last two seconds of the break are weaker, and on ESPN a game sample outscores every return window.

Adding later breaks to the library does not create a repeat. The short sound at the cut is not a fingerprint that travels from one game to the next at the published bar.
